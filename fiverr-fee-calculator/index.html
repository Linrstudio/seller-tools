Fiverr's fees, piece by piece

The 20%. Fiverr takes a flat 20% of every completed order — gig price, Gig Extras and tips included, at every seller level. You earn 80% of the purchase amount; the fee is deducted before the money reaches your balance, so your dashboard earnings are already net. There is no lower tier to unlock and no category that pays less. A $100 gig leaves you $80; a $500 gig leaves you $400.

What the client pays. The buyer pays a separate service fee: 5.5% of the purchase amount, plus $3.50 on orders under $200. So a $100 gig costs the client $109 while you keep $80 — the spread between those two numbers is Fiverr's real take on the transaction. Quote accordingly: the client is not paying $100, they are paying $109.

Clearing period. Earnings sit in clearing for 14 days after order completion (7 days for Top Rated, Seller Plus Premium and Pro sellers) before you can withdraw them. Price rush jobs with that delay in mind.

Withdrawal. PayPal is free from Fiverr's side (minimum $1, usually within 24 hours). Bank transfer via Payoneer is listed at $1 on Fiverr's main withdrawal page — but its Payoneer setup guide lists $3 for local-currency transfers and $1 for a USD wire, so check the fee shown before you confirm; this calculator uses $1. A Payoneer account costs $3 (minimum $10). Fiverr caps withdrawals at $5,000 per 24 hours, one withdrawal a day. Currency conversion markups from PayPal or Payoneer sit on top of these fees and are not in this calculator — they vary by country and provider.

Upwork is no longer a flat 10%

This is the fact most Upwork calculators still get wrong. Since May 2025, the freelancer service fee is 0% to 15% per contract — set when the contract starts, shown to you before you accept, and locked for the life of that contract. Contracts from before May 2025 keep their 10% flat rate. The field above defaults to 10%: change it to the rate on your contract, because a 15% contract pays $850 on $1,000 where a 10% one pays $900.

Connects. Proposals cost $0.15 per Connect. They are the cost of winning the job, so they belong in the math — ten Connects to land a $1,000 contract is another $1.50 off your take.

What the client pays. Your client pays a 5% marketplace fee on the Basic plan, plus a one-time contract initiation fee of $0.99–$14.99 per new contract. On a $1,000 contract at 10%, the client pays $1,050 plus initiation while you keep $900.

Withdrawal. Direct to a US bank is free for freelancers with a US tax address; since 1 September 2026 it costs $2.99 per transfer for freelancers whose tax address is outside the US. Direct to a local bank is $0.99, PayPal and Payoneer $2, Instant Pay (US) $2, and a US dollar wire $50. Direct Contracts — where you bring your own client onto Upwork for payment protection — cost 5%, or 0% with Freelancer Plus.

Fiverr vs Upwork at a glance

On a $1,000 job: Fiverr keeps $200 and you keep $800, while the client pays $1,055. Upwork at 10% keeps $100 and you keep $900, while the client pays $1,050 plus the initiation fee. Upwork leaves more per dollar — but you pay Connects to win the work, the rate varies by contract, and the client sees a marketplace fee too. Run your real numbers above; the cheaper platform is the one where your rate, your Connects spend and your withdrawal route add up smallest.

FAQ

How much does Fiverr take from a $100 gig? $20 — you keep $80 before withdrawal fees. The 20% covers the whole order including extras and tips. The client pays $109 on that gig (5.5% + $3.50 under $200).

Is Upwork still 10%? Only for contracts started before May 2025. New contracts carry a 0–15% freelancer service fee, set per contract and locked in. Check the rate on your offer before you accept — it follows the contract for its whole life.

Should I raise my price to cover the fee? That is what reverse mode is for: enter the take-home pay you want and it tells you the price to charge. On Fiverr, wanting $800 means charging $1,000; wanting $80 means charging $100. The fee is a percentage, so it scales — there is no price where it disappears.
